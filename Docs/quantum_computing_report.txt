Quantum Computing Hardware Review 2025
========================================
Authors: Dr. Pavel Novak, Dr. Sarah Chen
Institute: Quantum Research Consortium
Date: July 2025

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report reviews the current state of quantum computing hardware,
focusing on superconducting qubits, trapped ion systems, and photonic
quantum processors. We evaluate coherence times, gate fidelities,
and error correction progress across leading platforms.

QUBIT TECHNOLOGIES COMPARISON

Superconducting Qubits (IBM, Google)
- Qubit count: up to 1,121 (IBM Condor)
- Gate fidelity: 99.5 percent two-qubit gates
- Coherence time: 100-500 microseconds
- Operating temperature: 15 millikelvin
- Error rate per gate: 0.1-0.5 percent

Trapped Ion Systems (IonQ, Quantinuum)
- Qubit count: up to 32 fully connected
- Gate fidelity: 99.9 percent two-qubit gates
- Coherence time: seconds to minutes
- Operating temperature: room temperature (ion trap)
- Error rate per gate: 0.01-0.1 percent

Photonic Processors (PsiQuantum, Xanadu)
- Qubit count: variable (measurement-based)
- Gate fidelity: 99.3 percent
- Coherence time: nanoseconds (photon lifetime)
- Operating temperature: room temperature
- Advantage: scalable silicon fabrication

QUANTUM ERROR CORRECTION MILESTONES
- Google Willow: below threshold error correction achieved (Dec 2024)
- IBM: 1000-qubit logical error rate below physical error rate
- Microsoft: topological qubit prototype demonstrated (Q1 2025)

QUANTUM ADVANTAGE DEMONSTRATIONS
- Optimization: 10,000x speedup on portfolio optimization vs classical
- Chemistry: molecular simulation of FeMoco (nitrogen fixation enzyme)
- Cryptography: Shor algorithm run on 2048-bit RSA key (simulation)
- Machine learning: quantum kernel methods on 512-dimensional feature space

INDUSTRY APPLICATIONS TIMELINE
2025-2026: Quantum chemistry, drug discovery simulations
2027-2028: Financial optimization, logistics routing
2029-2030: Cryptographically relevant quantum computers (CRQC)
Post-2030: Fault-tolerant universal quantum computation

CONCLUSION
Quantum computing is transitioning from research to early commercial use.
Hardware progress is accelerating but fault-tolerant general-purpose
quantum computing remains 5-10 years away.
